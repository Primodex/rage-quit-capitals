America's Rage-Quit Capitals

Ranking 50 U.S. cities by how well their internet holds up for online gaming

A fast internet plan sounds like the cure for laggy games, but it isn't always enough. Many players pay for top speeds only to find that out in the middle of a match, when their screen freezes and the other team wins. Whether your connection is ready for gaming depends on where you live and what your internet actually delivers.

To see where lag hits hardest, Compare Internet scored 50 U.S. cities on seven measures that determine whether a game feels smooth: ping, download and upload speeds, how steady ping is, fiber options, provider choice, and how often people search for lag fixes.

Milwaukee, Detroit, and Louisville are America's top three rage-quit capitals, with Milwaukee ranked the worst of all. The biggest cities, on the other hand, aren't the worst places to play. Knowing how your city stacks up makes it easier to look past the speed in an ad and compare plans that hold up when the match is on the line.

Key Takeaways

  • Milwaukee, Detroit, and Louisville are America's top three rage-quit capitals, with the worst gaming internet of the 50 cities.
  • Colorado Springs, Fort Worth, and Mesa are the calmest places to game, with the best scores of the 50.
  • Detroit, Dallas, and Phoenix have the slowest typical measured download speeds.
  • Milwaukee, Long Beach, and New York have the least steady ping from month to month.
  • Minneapolis, Las Vegas, and Seattle search for gaming lag the most for cities their size.
  • Louisville, Kansas City, and Nashville have the highest ping, so data takes the longest to reach a server and come back.
  • Louisville, Detroit, and Dallas have the slowest typical measured upload speeds.
  • Tampa, El Paso, and Louisville have the fewest fiber providers per ZIP code.
  • El Paso, Wichita, and Louisville give homes the fewest internet providers to choose from per ZIP code.

The Midwest has the worst ping

The typical Midwest city sits at 28 ms, compared with 17 ms across all 50 cities. Six of the nine Midwest cities rank in the top 20 rage-quit capitals. That is 67%, the highest share of any region.

The South crowds the top of the list

Five of the top 10 rage-quit capitals are in the South: Louisville, Jacksonville, Tampa, Baltimore, and Atlanta. The South is also home to seven of the 14 cities with fast plans but laggy connections. The West has four, the Midwest three, and the Northeast none.

The region is split, though. Fort Worth, Austin, and Arlington, all in Texas, rank among the five cities with the best internet for gaming.

Most Western cities steer clear of the top 20

The West has the second-lowest typical ping of any region, at 13 ms. Only 28% of its cities (five of 18) rank in the top 20. Oakland, at 19th, is the only California city in the top 20. The other eight rank 21st or lower.

The Northeast has the smoothest connections

The Northeast has the lowest typical ping (7 ms) and the best typical score of any region, and none of its cities rank in the top 20. Only three Northeast cities are in the study: Boston, New York, and Philadelphia.

Methodology

To find the U.S. cities with the worst internet for gaming, we built a meta-ranking of 50 U.S. cities. Each city was scored on seven measures that decide whether a game feels smooth. All seven carry equal weight, about 14% each.

Connection Quality (4 of 7 measures, about 57% of the score)

Using M-Lab's public speed test data from October 2024 to September 2026 (monthly figures for each city, averaged by number of tests), we measured four things for each city, each worth about 14%:

  • Ping: how long data takes to reach a server and come back. Lower is better.
  • Typical measured download: the typical download speed in real tests. Typical measured speeds sit far below the gigabit plans offered because most homes are on slower plans, home tests often run over Wi-Fi, and the M-Lab test uses a single connection.
  • Typical measured upload: the typical upload speed in real tests.
  • Ping swing: how much typical ping changes from month to month. A smaller swing is better.

Provider Access (2 of 7 measures, about 29% of the score)

Using CompareInternet's ZIP-level provider data, we measured two things, each worth about 14%:

  • Fiber options: the average number of fiber providers per ZIP code.
  • Provider choice: the average number of internet providers per ZIP code, with satellite excluded.

Each city's figure is a plain average across its ZIP codes. For New York, the provider file covers Manhattan only. We used FCC National Broadband Map data (December 2025) for the whole city on these two measures instead.

Lag Searches (1 of 7 measures, about 14% of the score)

We used Google search volume for 147 gaming and internet terms in each city, covering October 2022 to September 2026. The lag score uses 90 lag, ping, and packet-loss terms, such as "Fortnite lag" and "why is my ping so high," from the most recent 12 months (October 2025 to September 2026). Big cities search more just because more people live there, so each city's searches are compared with what we would expect for a city of its size. Four months with national game outages were removed (November 2025, December 2025, March 2026, May 2026). Of the 147 terms, 128 returned data. Of the 90 lag terms, 81 had data.

Final Ranking

Each measure was converted to a score showing how far a city sits from the average of the 50 cities. The seven scores were then averaged into one rage-quit score. Cities were ranked from worst (1) to best (50).

To test the ranking, we re-ran it eight ways, including different weights and a version without the size adjustment. The top cities stayed near the top in most runs.

Fast plans, laggy connections

We mark a city as "fast plans, laggy connection" when it is in the top quarter of the 50 for gigabit plans offered (FCC) or typical measured download, and in the worst quarter for ping, fiber options, or lag searches. 14 cities qualify.

What this study can't tell you

  • The study measures cities, not individual players.
  • M-Lab ping measures the connection to a test server, not to a game server.
  • Search volume shows interest in lag, not its cause.
  • ZIP code averages treat small and large ZIP codes equally.
